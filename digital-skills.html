2. Computer Basics

A computer is an electronic device that receives data, processes it, stores it and produces information.

Main Computer Components

  • CPU: Processes instructions.
  • RAM: Temporary working memory.
  • Storage: Keeps files and applications.
  • Motherboard: Connects major components.
  • Input devices: Keyboard, mouse and scanner.
  • Output devices: Monitor, printer and speakers.

Operating System

An operating system manages computer hardware and provides an environment where applications can run.

Examples include Windows, Linux, macOS, Android and iOS.

Remember: Hardware is the physical part of a computer while software consists of programs and instructions.

3. Internet Basics

The Internet is a worldwide network of interconnected computer networks that communicate using standard protocols.

Important Internet Terms

  • Website: A collection of web pages.
  • Browser: Software used to access websites.
  • URL: Address of a web resource.
  • IP Address: Identifies a device on a network.
  • DNS: Converts domain names into IP addresses.
  • ISP: Provides Internet access.

Web Browsers

Popular browsers include Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari and Opera. 

https://www.example.com

HTTPS indicates that communication between the browser and website is protected using encryption.

4. Email & Online Communication

Email is one of the most important digital communication tools used by students, professionals and businesses.

Parts of an Email

  • Recipient
  • Subject
  • Message body
  • Attachment
  • Signature

Email Best Practices

  1. Use a clear subject.
  2. Use professional language.
  3. Check the recipient before sending.
  4. Do not open suspicious attachments.
  5. Review your message before sending.
Never share passwords, verification codes or sensitive information through suspicious emails.

5. Digital Information & Research

The Internet contains a huge amount of information. Digital literacy requires the ability to find, evaluate and use information responsibly.

Effective Search

  • Use specific keywords.
  • Use quotation marks for exact phrases.
  • Compare multiple sources.
  • Check the publication date.
  • Look for trustworthy authors and organizations.

Evaluating Information

Ask yourself:

  • Who created the information?
  • When was it published?
  • What evidence supports it?
  • Is another reliable source saying the same thing?
Good digital researchers do not believe everything they see online. They verify information.

9. Digital Security

Digital security protects devices, accounts, data and online identities from unauthorized access and attacks.

Strong Passwords

  • Use long passwords.
  • Avoid predictable information.
  • Use different passwords for important accounts.
  • Use a password manager where appropriate.

Multi-Factor Authentication

MFA adds another verification factor in addition to a password.

Common Threats

  • Phishing
  • Malware
  • Identity theft
  • Password attacks
  • Social engineering
Never give your password or verification code to someone simply because they claim to be from a company.

10. Online Banking & Digital Services

Digital services allow people to perform financial, government and business activities using computers and mobile devices.

Examples

  • Mobile banking
  • Online banking
  • Mobile money
  • Online shopping
  • Digital government services
  • Electronic payments

Safety Tips

  • Use official applications and websites.
  • Check the website address carefully.
  • Never share your PIN.
  • Never share OTP or verification codes.
  • Avoid financial transactions on suspicious networks.
Banks and legitimate service providers should not require you to reveal confidential passwords or PINs.
